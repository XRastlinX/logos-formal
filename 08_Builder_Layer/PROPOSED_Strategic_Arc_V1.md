## The Five Phases

### Phase 1 — First Contact (current)

Make the repository self-explanatory and the demonstrators single-command.  
Success = a stranger can understand the claim in seconds and run something real in minutes.  
This phase ends when the first external reactions exist.

### Phase 2 — First Friction

Someone outside the project tries to use the work for a real purpose and hits a limitation or a need.  
That friction is more valuable than any number of formal leaves. It tells you which part of the system people actually reach for (the permit boundary, the decay tracking, the Cybot pattern, the Git Theory locks, etc.).

### Phase 3 — First Real Adoption

One lab, one safety team, one independent engineer, or one serious hobbyist keeps using a piece of the system.  
At that point the work stops being a private formal achievement and becomes a living dependency. This is also the first moment a scoped commercial or support conversation becomes non-delusional.

### Phase 4 — Tooling Gravity

The parts that people keep using get hardened into actual tools (a Cylang-style checker, a permit-controller library, an autophagic-decay monitor, a Cybot starter kit).  
The formal canon remains open; the production-grade implementations can carry commercial terms if desired. This is where Cy-Egology stops being theory.

### Phase 5 — Institutional Touch

The separation of interpretation from authorization is recognized by people who already speak RATS, in-toto, ABAC, or high-assurance systems language.  
Citations, workshop invitations, grant conversations, or standards-adjacent discussion become possible. This phase is slow and not guaranteed, but it is only reachable if Phases 1–3 actually happened.

## What the Longer Game Requires

- Ruthless protection of the epistemic posture. One public over-claim can undo months of careful boundary work.
- Willingness to watch what outsiders do with the surface instead of pre-deciding what they should care about.
- Patience with the fact that the first useful signal will probably look small and slightly off-target.
- Readiness to turn the highest-friction piece into a concrete tool once real demand appears.
- Continued refusal to let the local agent or any external model self-elevate research boundaries.

## The Strategic Bet

The longer game is not "become a popular agent framework."  
It is "become the small, sharp, trusted reference for fail-closed separation of interpretation from authorization."

That is a narrower and more durable position. It only works if the public surface is excellent and the boundaries stay clean.

## Current Position

Phase 1 is in execution. Everything beyond it depends on whether first contact actually converts into first friction.
