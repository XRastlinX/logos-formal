# Cy-Egology Adoption Arc V1

**Record Type:** Epistemic Canon  
**Layer:** Adoption and Sustainability  
**Status:** ACTIVE_CANON  

## 1. Overview

The Cy-Egology Adoption Arc defines the rigorous phase transitions required for the `logos-formal` architecture to advance from theoretical proposal to active, sustained operational reality. It enforces anti-inflation discipline to ensure that perceived progress strictly matches empirical reality.

## 2. Phase Gates

### Phase 0: The Algebraic Baseline
*   **Criteria:** Mathematical scaffolding, defined algebraic domains, and exact byte representations of the core SysML constraints.
*   **Status:** Successfully mapped and quarantined.

### Phase 1: First Contact (External Verification)
*   **Criteria:** Must record **3** independent cold runs and **1** verified friction event (or formal charter change) from external actors.
*   **Status:** `EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (Awaiting distributed, independent execution).

### Phase 2: Autonomous Persistence
*   **Criteria:** The system operates a durable background state, managing cyclic workflows via the CyExchange corridor without continuous manual intervention.

## 3. Anti-Inflation Discipline

The repository is governed by strict truth-in-advertising constraints:
1.  **No Extrapolation:** Internal test runs, AI simulated interactions, and "maintainer" actions do not constitute external adoption.
2.  **Friction is Empirical:** External friction must be an observed, reproducible blockage reported by an independent participant. It cannot be mathematically simulated or waived by internal consensus.
3.  **Active Canon Promotion:** Theoretical blueprints remain `PROPOSED` until they meet their associated evidence gates. Only empirical satisfaction of the gate elevates a blueprint to `ACTIVE_CANON`.

## 4. Survival Criteria

The system's longevity relies on the actual operational friction it identifies and resolves. If the Phase 1 First Contact gate cannot be satisfied by empirical distribution, the adoption arc halts. The system must fail honestly rather than invent simulated success.
